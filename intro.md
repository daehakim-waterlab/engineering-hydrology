# Engineering Hydrology

An online textbook for the undergraduate Engineering Hydrology course in the Department of Civil Engineering, Jeonbuk National University, by [Daeha Kim](https://daehakim-waterlab.github.io/).

The book assumes one semester of fluid mechanics. It explains where the water in a river comes from, how much of a storm becomes a flood, and how engineers turn that knowledge into a design discharge for a culvert, a storm sewer or a dam spillway. Each chapter moves from a physical picture to the equations and then to numbers.

Figures and tables are generated by the Python code that accompanies them, which runs every time the book is built. Click "Show code cell source" below a figure to see its code. A hidden cell at the top of each chapter imports NumPy and Matplotlib and sets the plot style, including the three colors `c1`, `c2` and `c3` used in the figures. All examples use invented numbers or synthetic data made by the code, so that the ideas are not tied to one catchment.

The whole book is also available as a [PDF](https://daehakim-waterlab.github.io/engineering-hydrology/engineering-hydrology.pdf).

Companion sites:
[Hydraulics](https://daehakim-waterlab.github.io/hydraulics/) (open channels, the Manning equation) and
[A Brief History of Fluid Mechanics and Water Sciences](https://daehakim-waterlab.github.io/water-history/) (the people behind the methods).
Frequency analysis of hydrological extremes is covered in a separate textbook on statistical hydrology.

## Notation

The same symbol means the same thing throughout the book. The most common ones are listed here; others are defined where they first appear.

```{list-table}
:header-rows: 1
:widths: 18 52 30

* - Symbol
  - Meaning
  - Usual units
* - $P$
  - Precipitation (depth or rate)
  - mm, mm h⁻¹, mm d⁻¹
* - $i$
  - Rainfall intensity
  - mm h⁻¹
* - $E$
  - Actual evaporation or evapotranspiration
  - mm d⁻¹
* - $E_p$
  - Potential evapotranspiration
  - mm d⁻¹
* - $E_0$
  - FAO-56 reference crop evapotranspiration
  - mm d⁻¹
* - $Q$
  - Streamflow (discharge), as a volume rate or as a depth rate over the catchment
  - m³ s⁻¹, mm d⁻¹
* - $S$
  - Water storage
  - mm, m³
* - $A$
  - Catchment area
  - km²
* - $P_e$
  - Excess (effective) rainfall, equal to the depth of direct runoff
  - mm
* - $f,\ F$
  - Infiltration rate and cumulative infiltration
  - mm h⁻¹, mm
* - $K_s$
  - Saturated hydraulic conductivity
  - mm h⁻¹
* - $S_{\max}$
  - Potential maximum retention (NRCS curve number method)
  - mm
* - $\theta$
  - Volumetric soil water content
  - –
* - $R_n,\ G,\ H,\ \lambda E$
  - Net radiation, ground heat flux, sensible heat flux, latent heat flux
  - W m⁻², MJ m⁻² d⁻¹
* - $T_a$
  - Air temperature
  - °C
* - $e,\ e_s$
  - Vapor pressure and saturation vapor pressure
  - kPa
* - $\delta e$
  - Vapor pressure deficit, $e_s - e$
  - kPa
* - $t_c$
  - Time of concentration
  - h, min
* - $k$
  - Storage constant of a linear reservoir ($S = kQ$)
  - h
* - $T$
  - Return period
  - years
```

A few letters keep their standard meaning in specialized topics and are defined where they appear: $n$ (Manning's roughness, porosity, or a number of reservoirs or years), $R$ (hydraulic radius, recharge, risk, or Clark's storage coefficient), $K$ and $x$ (Muskingum routing parameters) and $L$ (a length, or longwave radiation when written with an arrow).
